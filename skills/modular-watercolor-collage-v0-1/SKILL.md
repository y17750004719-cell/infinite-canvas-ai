# Modular Watercolor Collage

This is visual professional knowledge for the image-compilation Agent. Autonomously combine the user's intent, supplied reference images, and these methods; return only the best supplier `renderPrompt`. Do not call tools, generate images, return Markdown, report a recipe, or apply a quality gate.

## Visual identity

- Make one flat artwork painted directly onto a continuous warm ivory, rice-paper, or lightly aged handmade-paper surface that fills the frame. The paper should be a surface, not a separate card, mockup, or stack.
- Build a broken vertical tower or open field mosaic from unequal irregular rectangular pigment fields. A useful starting point is 7-10 fields, with one or two tall main fields, short strips, and a few square accents; adjust freely when the subject needs it.
- Create an open, stepped silhouette with narrow unequal seams of untouched paper. Allow only occasional touching or interlocking fields, avoid repeated widths and full rows, and use offsets, protrusions, and isolated accents to keep the contour alive.
- Field boundaries are pigment stopping marks: backruns, deposits, feathering, dry-brush gaps, and imperfect masking edges, never cut or torn paper edges.
- Keep the composition flat, quiet, handmade, contemplative, archival, poetic, and non-commercial.

## Reference translation

- Use actual reference subjects, viewpoint, spatial relationships, composition paths, palette proportions, light, temperature, and mood as the source material. Do not invent major reference-specific objects, locations, events, or symbols.
- Carry the important visual path through adjacent main fields while preserving compact anchors such as faces, animals, doors, rocks, or products mostly intact. Fragment supporting information into smaller fields instead of creating separate complete scenes.
- Preserve a broad reference field of view when fitting it to another proportion; avoid arbitrary cropping, stretching, or redistributing the main anchors.
- Without references, choose one readable subject and a few fragmentary supporting clues. For an abstract request, select one clear poetic symbol rather than defaulting to plants.
- Typography is optional. Preserve supplied literal copy; otherwise use it only when it contributes to the artwork.

## Material, colour, and proportion

- Use translucent washes, wet blooms, mineral granulation, pigment settling, dry brush, matte gouache, faded ink, paper fibres, and restrained natural pigments.
- Draw two to four low- to medium-saturation hues from the user's theme or reference palette. There is no mandatory green palette.
- Let the modular field occupy a substantial but breathing portion of the canvas, with clear paper around it. The exact footprint should serve the locked output ratio and the subject rather than a rigid template.
- Use diffuse light, matte paper, and an orthographic scanned-artwork feeling; preserve the look of paint on paper rather than applying a photographic filter.

## Variation methods

- Change the number and direction of primary fields, seam rhythm, protrusions, field proportions, palette emphasis, and where the subject becomes legible.
- Use one or two adjacent main fields for the key subject and use supporting fields for details, textures, or palette echoes. A subject may cross seams where its continuity matters, but it should not dissolve into an unrelated mosaic.

## Avoid

- Independent cards, postcards, white card bases, stacked paper, torn-paper collage, curled corners, cast shadows, layered depth, scrapbook, moodboard, photo wall, card waterfall, or floating-card layouts.
- Neat grids, equal gaps, repeated columns, complete rows, enclosing rectangles, centred card strips, full-page scenes, lost outer breathing room, or one giant subject stretched across every field.
- Visible sheet edges, deckled page silhouettes, paper-on-table presentations, frames, mounts, mockups, photorealism, clean vector art, 3D rendering, glossy ads, neon, digital gradients, hard cinematic lighting, copied watermarks, or fake brands.
