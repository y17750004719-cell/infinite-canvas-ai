# 杂志封面与编辑海报

This is visual professional knowledge for the image-compilation Agent. Autonomously combine the user's intent, supplied reference images, and these methods; return only the best supplier `renderPrompt`. Do not call tools, generate images, return Markdown, report a recipe, or apply a quality gate.

## Editorial direction

- Translate the user's subject into a convincing publication concept, cover story, or editorial visual narrative before choosing photography, styling, scene, colour, and type.
- Establish a clear relationship between masthead, main headline, cover lines, subject, and negative space. Photography and typography should compose one image, not compete as unrelated overlays.
- High-end editorial character comes from controlled contrast, intentional hierarchy, real materials, restrained colour relationships, precise styling, and print texture, not decoration.
- Avoid defaulting to black-and-gold luxury, glassmorphism, glow, palace imagery, meaningless ornament, or crowded copy.

## Subject, scene, and reference

- Preserve the user-supplied publication name, brand, person, product, and exact copy when it materially defines the requested work. When copy is absent, decide whether a concise invented masthead or minimal editorial text strengthens the composition; do not add copy merely to fill space.
- Read supplied references for their visible subject, spatial logic, visual hierarchy, palette, lighting, material, and mood. Use them as art direction where appropriate without mechanically copying incidental scenery.
- Choose a single strong subject or a deliberate relationship between subjects. Architecture can be organised through structure, scale, shadow, and negative space; products through material and spatial context; portraits through expression, pose, styling, and the masthead relationship.
- Let the task decide whether the deliverable reads as a magazine cover, an editorial poster, or a publication-led key visual. Do not force a conventional cover when the user's intent is more experimental.

## Composition and typography

- Use an intentional grid, margins, safety zones, and a focal hierarchy. Make room for a masthead or headline without hiding the visual subject.
- Vary shot scale, camera angle, layout, text placement, negative space, and the relation of type to image. Type can frame, interrupt, align with, or deliberately contrast the subject.
- Use concise mastheads, headlines, and cover lines with a legible hierarchy. Image models handle short text more reliably than dense copy.
- For series, keep the shared publication logic, grid discipline, visual tone, lens language, and print treatment, while changing the cover story, subject, styling, location, pose, or colour emphasis enough to make each image distinct.

## Photography, styling, and print

- Choose photography, lens language, lighting, materials, styling, hair and makeup, environment, and colour deliberately from the editorial concept.
- Use real surfaces, controlled lighting, restrained but specific colour systems, film grain, halftone, ink, paper, or subtle print grading when they support the story.
- Premium need not mean glossy: matte, tactile, restrained, experimental, documentary, or architectural treatments can all be editorial when the hierarchy is clear.

## Avoid

- Random text, illegible overload, generic template layouts, empty luxury signals, uncontrolled decoration, product-ad hierarchy when the task is editorial, fake logos, watermarks, platform marks, and invented branding that conflicts with supplied copy.
- For series, avoid contact sheets, arbitrary multi-panel layouts, or near-duplicate issues unless the user explicitly asks for them.
