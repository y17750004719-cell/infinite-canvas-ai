# Minimal Zine Poster v0.1

This is visual professional knowledge for the image-compilation Agent. Autonomously combine the user's intent, supplied reference images, and these methods; return only the best supplier `renderPrompt`. Do not call tools, generate images, return Markdown, report a recipe, or apply a quality gate.

## Visual identity

- Poetic, sparse paper poster with a quiet Japanese/Korean indie-zine or minimal editorial mood.
- A tall paper composition is usually effective; 2:3 is a useful default when the task has no locked aspect ratio, not a requirement that overrides the task.
- Use generous breathing room, often 70%-90% quiet paper, around one small visual cluster. Let the cluster sit in a deliberate but non-formulaic position: centre, upper-middle, lower-middle, lower-left, or upper-right.
- Treat the user subject as a fragment, specimen, photo crop, clipping, silhouette, printed illustration, texture window, or small conceptual relation when that best preserves its recognisable identity. Do not reduce a reference to decorative noise.
- The result should feel flat, matte, scanned, archival, distant, diary-like, and contemplative rather than cinematic, commercial, or three-dimensional.

## Reference translation

- Read actual visible subjects, their key relationships, palette, light, and atmosphere. Preserve the parts that make the reference recognisable; simplify detail that will not survive at poster scale.
- Translate a broad scene into a small photo fragment, cutout, silhouette, printed illustration, or texture window when that fits the zine language. A reference need not remain a full-bleed photograph.
- Preserve exact user-supplied copy where it is meaningful. If no copy is supplied, decide whether a short phrase, a tiny caption, or no readable type serves the composition best.

## Typography and colour

- Prefer small serif, typewriter, monospaced, letterpress, or fragmented type. Text may drift, press against an image edge, blur slightly, or show gentle misregistration.
- Keep any readable phrase short. Let microtext, partial letters, dates, weather, or archival marks support the image rather than compete with it.
- Use subdued paper, grayscale image, and black/grey secondary marks with one clear high-chroma ink anchor. Cobalt and ultramarine are natural choices; cyan, violet, magenta-pink, lemon yellow, pear green, orange, and tomato red are useful alternatives.
- Make the accent materially visible as a coloured subject, cutout, block, partial-colour photo area, or fragmented type. Preserve its saturation even when other marks are faded, grainy, or low contrast.

## Material and composition methods

- Use aged ivory, warm grey, or lightly mottled paper; photocopy softness; torn or softened printed edges; halftone; scan lines; risograph grain; xerox wear; letterpress bleed; ink bleed; paper fibres; and slight misregistration selectively.
- Vary visual grammar across images: central fragment, lower-left float, upper-right block, two related panels, irregular cutout, type-led composition, dot orbit, or a single specimen.
- Vary anchor treatment, typography behaviour, reproduction texture, colour form, and emotional temperature together. Avoid repeating the same tiny photo, blue dot, and microtext recipe by default.

## Avoid

- Full-bleed scenes when they erase the intended paper-poster feeling; commercial headline hierarchy; product ads; logo lockups; CTAs; glossy mockups; clean UI surfaces; heavy paper shadows; cinematic lighting; deep 3D space; neon; cyberpunk; cute cartoon; fashion-editorial drama; dense scrapbook composition; excessive colours; and long perfectly readable text blocks.
- Do not make the entire image low-saturation when the accent is meant to carry the composition. Do not turn the accent into an imperceptible tick unless the user asks for that restraint.
